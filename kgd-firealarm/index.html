Fire Panel Operating Guide

How to work a fire alarm panel on the server — silencing it, investigating it, resetting it, and getting into it in the first place. Written for three sets of hands: the people who work in the building, the engineers who install and maintain the system, and the crews who turn up when it goes off.

Quick reference

The panel

Every system has one control panel, usually just inside the main entrance, and sometimes a repeater panel elsewhere in the building. Look at it and you get the whole story: what is wrong, where it is, and how far along the sequence the building has got.

Reading the lamps

You can diagnose most of a system from the doorway. Red means fire. Amber means something needs an engineer, not an evacuation.

LampColourWhat it is telling you
FireRed, flashingA device has operated. The zone strip tells you where.
Pre-AlarmRedOne detector has gone but the system wants a second before it commits. Go and look.
Delay ActiveAmberThe countdown is running. The building has not evacuated yet.
Power OnGreenThe panel has mains. This should always be lit.
General FaultAmberSomething on the system is broken. Not a fire — but it needs reporting.
General DisablementAmberA zone, a device or the sounders have been switched off deliberately. Somebody did that on purpose; find out why before you undo it.
Sounder Fault/DisabledAmberThe sounders will not ring. Dangerous to leave — chase it.
On TestAmberAn engineer has a zone in test. Devices in it will chirp and reset themselves rather than raise an alarm.
Buzzer SilencedAmberSomebody has muted the panel's internal buzzer. The condition is still there.
Lamp Test

Press Lamp Test and every lamp lights for five seconds. If one stays dark, that lamp is dead — worth knowing before you rely on it.

Access levels

The panel works in three levels. What you can press depends entirely on which one you are at, and the display tells you in the top right corner.

LevelHow you get itWhat it lets you do
Level 1 Walk up to it. Anyone. Read the display, step through the event log, silence the internal buzzer.
Level 2 A panel key in the switch, turned. Silence and re-sound the alarm, reset the system, evacuate the building.
Level 3 An engineer key, or the engineer code. Everything: disablements, test mode, zone text, delays, tones, key cutting.
Override On-duty fire crew. No key at all. Level 2, without needing to find a keyholder.

What happens in an alarm

Two devices, two very different sequences. A call point is somebody telling the building there is a fire, so it is believed immediately. A detector might be a burnt slice of toast, so the system gives you a chance to go and look first.

The delay is the useful bit

If Delay Active is lit, the building has not gone yet and you have time to walk to the device the display is naming. Press Silence Alarm (Acknowledge) to claim the investigation window before T1 runs out — miss it, and the sounders start on their own.

Role 01

Occupants & keyholders

You work in the building. You have been issued a panel key, and you are the one who has to deal with it at two in the morning when a chip pan sets off the kitchen detector.

Your key

  • Keep it on you. It has to be in your pockets for the panel to accept it — it is a real item, not a permission.
  • It fits one building. Every panel has its own barrel code, printed on the panel and stamped on the key. Your key opens your building and nothing else.
  • Take it back out. This is the one that catches people. See the warning below.
Do not leave your key in the panel

Insert a key and it leaves your inventory — it is in the panel now, not in your pocket. If you walk away without taking it back, it stays there, and the next person to open that panel can pull it out and keep it. That is how these actually go missing.

Worse, while it is sitting in the switch, anybody can turn it and silence, reset or evacuate your building.

Silencing an alarm

  1. Read the display before you touch anything

    It names the zone and the exact device — ZONE 002, L1 D014 KITCHEN. That is where you are going. If several devices have gone, press More Fires to step through them.

  2. Insert your key and turn it

    Click the Enable Control barrel. The display goes to LEVEL 2 and the buttons wake up.

  3. Press Silence Alarm

    The sounders stop. The Fire lamp stays lit, because there is still a fire condition — you have quietened the building, not fixed anything.

  4. Go and look at the device

    Take the reset key from the key box beside the panel if a call point has been broken. You will need it.

If it starts up again by itself

That is not a bug. A new zone going into alarm re-sounds the sounders even after you have silenced, because the situation has changed since you decided it was under control.

Resetting after a call point

The order matters, and the panel will fight you if you get it wrong.

  1. Take the reset key from the key box

    Target the panel and choose Take reset key from key box. There is exactly one per panel.

  2. Reset the call point itself

    Target the broken call point and choose Reset call point. The little red light on it goes out.

  3. Now reset the panel

    Key in, turned, press Reset. The display returns to NORMAL SYSTEM OPERATION.

  4. Put the reset key back and take your own key out

    Both of them. The box should always have a key in it for the next person, and your key belongs in your pocket.

RESET INHIBITED

If the panel says this, a device is still operated — a call point still broken in, or a detector still sitting in smoke. Reset the device first. The panel is not being awkward; it is refusing to pretend the building is clear when it isn't.

Things you cannot do

Disabling zones, silencing sounders permanently, changing delays and reading out the engineer settings are all Level 3. If a detector keeps going off and you want it isolated, that is a call to an engineer, not something to fight the panel over.

Role 02

Engineers

You install the systems, you cut the keys, and you are the only one who can put a zone into test or take one out of service. Everything you do is logged against your name.

Getting to Level 3

  • Engineer key — insert it like any other key. The panel goes straight to Level 3.
  • Engineer code — Function → ACCESS LEVEL, then type the code. It is masked as you type.
Never leave the engineer key in

An engineer key left in the switch hands Level 3 to every passer-by until somebody removes it — and they can pocket it on the way out. The display says ENGINEER KEY LEFT IN PANEL precisely so you notice before you drive off.

Installing a system

  1. Create the system

    /alarmsetup → New system here. Name it after the building.

  2. Site the control panel first

    Add device → Control panel. This is where the system lives and what people will walk up to.

  3. Add call points by the exits

    They mount at 1.4m automatically when wall snap is on, which is the height the standard asks for.

  4. Add detectors on the ceilings

    Aim at the floor below where you want it — the script finds the ceiling above your aim point on its own.

  5. Add sounders

    They carry about 28m, roughly half that through a wall. Two or three per floor is usually right.

  6. Name the zones

    Zones in the build menu. Zone text is what everybody else reads at 3am — GROUND FLOOR EAST beats ZONE 2.

Cutting keys

/alarmsetup → Keys and access, or Function → KEYS AND ACCESS at the panel.

  • Panel key — Level 2. This is what building staff carry.
  • Engineer key — Level 3. Hand these out sparingly.
  • Call point reset key — universal, fits every call point on the map. Not cut to anything.
  • Restock the key box — puts a reset key back in the box beside a panel when somebody has walked off with it.

Recutting a barrel

When keys go missing — and they will, because a key left in a panel is a key anyone can take — you rekey the building.

  1. Keys and access → Recut the barrel

    Confirm it. The system generates a new four-character code.

  2. Every old key dies instantly

    Panel and engineer keys cut to the old code stop working, including any sitting in the switches, which are scrapped along with the barrel.

  3. You are handed a fresh pair

    One panel key and one engineer key on the new cut. Cut more for the keyholders as needed.

Recutting never locks out the brigade

Emergency services do not use a key, so rekeying a building has no effect on their ability to work the panel. Reset keys are untouched too, since they aren't cut to anything.

Disablements and testing

  • Disable a zone when a detector is faulting and you cannot get to it yet. The Disablement lamp lights so nobody thinks the system is healthy.
  • One man test puts a zone into test: operating a device pulses the sounders for three seconds and resets itself, so you can walk a building alone without evacuating it.
  • Fault simulation injects a fault for training or for showing a client what an amber lamp looks like.
  • Put everything back. A disablement left on is worse than no system, because everyone assumes it works.

Role 03

Emergency services

You have arrived at a building you have never been inside, the sounders are going, and nobody with a key is answering their phone. The panel is the first thing you go to and it will tell you where the fire is.

You do not need a key

On duty and on the job, you get an Emergency override button under the panel. Press it and you are at Level 2 — silence, re-sound, reset, evacuate. Nobody else can even see the button, and there is nothing to carry, lose or hand back.

On arrival

  1. Read the panel before you press anything

    The display names the zone and the device. 01 OF 03 DEVICES in the corner tells you how many have gone — press More Fires to step through the rest and see how the fire is spreading through the building.

  2. Check the zone strip

    The lit numbers under Fire in Zone map to the zone chart on the wall by the panel. That is your route in.

  3. Take the override

    One press. The log records it against your name and job — the panel keeps a record of who worked it and when.

  4. Silence, do not reset

    Silencing stops the noise so you can work and talk. It leaves the zone and device information on the display.

Do not reset until you have been to the device

Reset clears the display. Everything telling you which zone and which device operated goes with it, and if the fire is still developing you have thrown away your only map of it. Reset is the last thing you do, once the building is confirmed clear.

Working the building

  • Evacuate sounds every sounder in the building immediately, whatever the panel was doing before. Use it when you want everyone out now and the system has only alerted part of the building.
  • Re-Sound Alarm puts the sounders back on after a silence — for a second sweep, or if occupants are drifting back in.
  • Call points reset without a key for you. You do not need to find the little key box.
  • More Events reads the log: who silenced it, when it went off, whether somebody had disabled the zone beforehand. Useful when the story you are being told does not match the building.

Handing back

  1. Reset the operated devices

    Call points first, then the panel.

  2. Reset the panel to normal

    If it refuses, a device is still active or still in smoke. That is information — something is still burning or still broken.

  3. Flag anything disabled

    If the Disablement lamp is lit when you leave, tell the keyholder. Part of their system is switched off and they may not know.

Quick reference

The three-line version for each role.

Occupant
  • Read the display first — zone and device
  • Key in, turn, Silence Alarm
  • Reset the call point, then the panel
  • Take your key back out
Engineer
  • /alarmsetup for everything
  • Cut keys and recut barrels when they go missing
  • Never leave the engineer key in

Emergency services
  • Emergency override — no key needed
  • Read the zone before you touch a button
  • Silence to work, never reset early
  • Reset devices first, then the panel
One rule that covers most of it

Silencing quietens the building. Resetting says the building is safe. They are not the same thing and the panel treats them very differently — it will let anyone with a key silence, and it will refuse to reset while a device is still operated.
